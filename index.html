01 · Development finance · 2024 – present

SmartEquity — one platform for 200+ development projects

InDome Capital, UK. Project analysis, due diligence and investor reporting lived in separate spreadsheets and inboxes; every deal was analysed from scratch.

Built
A Smartsheet-based platform covering the whole lifecycle, Origination → Delivery → Exit: origination register with automated appraisal and indicative term sheet; a data-collection system for delivery: a separate sheet per project for the budget, the work programme (with conditions tracker) and risks, plus registers for marketing plan, minutes and lessons learnt, all rolled up through reports into one dashboard that shows the overall health of the project; exit calculations (actual profit, redemption, buy-to-let scenario); investor dashboards and reports assembled from project data. The system is not only for internal use: developers run their projects in it, estate agents keep the marketing plan and price control, banks see the financial reporting, and investors get the same live dashboard, so every party works in one platform on one set of data. I am the author of the system: designed and built from scratch as Head of Operations, reporting to the owner; three project managers now run their projects in it.
Result
200+ projects in origination and 10 in delivery, £20M+ of projects on the platform. Project analysis 2–3 h → 40 min; at 1–2 analyses a day that frees roughly 30–100 hours of project-manager time a month (estimate). Investor report 1 h → 10 min. One source of truth for monitoring and reporting across the company.

02 · Property developer & landlord · Feb – Jun 2026

Fishbone — from manual Excel to an end-to-end management system

UK developer with a rental portfolio. The owners came to me because they wanted to scale: everything ran by hand, and they saw that growth was impossible without automating it. Sourcing, budgets and compliance were tracked in spreadsheets; profitability was estimated, not calculated.

Built
Working directly with the owners, a three-module Smartsheet system. Sourcing: deal register with automated appraisal per property. Delivery: a 1,200-item cost classifier with material and labour rates (a budget is assembled by entering item codes, and the rates are pulled in automatically), plan-vs-actual budget template, project database auto-filled from sourcing, risk and lessons-learnt registers, work programme. Property management: portfolio register with compliance tracking (gas, EPC, EICR, deposits, right-to-rent, insurance), quarterly inspection checklists and actions.
Result
Project profitability recalculated; costing errors and unaccounted works found. Appraisal and reporting 2× faster. Full transparency for the owners. Handed over and completed in four months. Afterwards the owners used the system to introduce AI agents: three positions were made redundant and the monthly payroll cost fell from £10,000 to £200, the price of an AI subscription.

03 · Engineering & design company · Sep 2026 – present

ROSECO — a regulations navigator, then the company's AI model

A repeat engagement: I came back to the company in September 2026 after a break, having worked there in 2019–2024. I came in to digitalise the company end to end and prepare it for AI agents in day-to-day operations. The regulations navigator was only the first pilot: engineers spent a large share of their time searching internal regulations and standards, and the company had no safe way to start with AI. The larger work behind it is preparing the infrastructure and setting up the company's processes so that AI agents can work in them.

Built
Working alongside the CEO. In three weeks: compared five AI tools against four guard-rails (document status, scope boundary, source citation, no invented facts), then built a bot inside Bitrix24 (CRM/workflow platform) with the people who use it. The "document assistant": vector index of 70 current regulations (1,609 fragments), answers with the document's code, name and status; runs on the Bitrix24 model, so no data leaves the company. Then the groundwork for AI in day-to-day operations: process maps, AI-use scenarios by function, and an anonymisation policy for cloud LLMs defining which data may leave the company.
Result
Document search 2× faster, measured on 5 users by time tracking. In the first three days 7 employees asked 101 questions; median answer 6.5 s; 100% of answers on indexed documents cite the source and its status. Side result: the bot surfaced 27 ambiguities in 7 process diagrams (3 high-priority), now with the process owners. The company's LLM-based knowledge model and rollout infrastructure are in progress.

How I work

Direct work with the owner, 18 years of construction knowledge from the inside, and a systematic, structured approach. Nothing is built before the process is drawn, and nothing counts as done until the team runs it without me.

STEP 1

Interview and audit

Sit down with the owner and the people who do the work. Draw AS-IS in BPMN, find the bottlenecks with Lean and Six Sigma tools (waste, delays, errors), and decide where automation or AI pays off and where it does not.

STEP 2

Design TO-BE with owner and team

The owner shows where the company is heading and which targets matter; the team shows the everyday operational pains. I work at both levels, with decision-makers and the people who do the work, and tie them into one target process with roles and approvals, agreed before anything is built.

STEP 3

Build in small increments, with the team

Scrum-style: I do not wait for the whole system to be ready. Work runs in short cycles: plan a small piece, build it, demo it to the team, collect feedback, adjust, then take the next piece. Each piece goes live and is tested before the next one is added.

STEP 4

Measure and hand over

Measure, improve and lock in the result on the real task (DMAIC logic), then leave a system the team runs without me. Where the work continues, I check after a few weeks whether it is still in use, and retire what is not.
